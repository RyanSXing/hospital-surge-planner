# Hospital operations and decision summary

Configuration adds an editable severity pie, a daily treatment disruption chance beside the arrivals chart, and one shared supply inventory. The settings are independent and optional. Existing saved scenarios keep their prior behavior until an operator enables or edits the new settings. Undo, redo, save, reload and simulation submission preserve these settings in the frozen revision.

Low, medium and high shares total 100%. Number controls, sliders, keyboard controls and draggable pie boundaries edit the same values. Each severity has an ordered positive mean inpatient bed stay. Newly arriving patients receive a severity and gamma-distributed duration from independent keyed random streams. In operations models 1.1–1.3, that duration scales only their inpatient and step-down care stages. Emergency and clinic assessments retain their separate baseline durations, and emergency-only visits do not receive an inpatient stay. Longer inpatient care keeps the relevant bed occupied longer. Queue, interruption and transport time are additional. Initial census patients retain their observed residual stays.

A disruption is an entire local-calendar day. Model 1.3 independently samples each local date using the configured 0–100% daily chance and a separate seeded random stream. Zero means no chance-generated interruptions and preserves prior random trajectories; 100% interrupts every day. Sampled days are reproducible within a run and may differ across repetitions. A local day can last 23 or 25 hours across daylight-saving changes; interruptions are clipped to the simulation horizon. Configuration shows the chance rather than invented disruption dates.

During a disruption, new arrivals continue but treatment starts, admissions and unfinished active-care completions stop. Patients retain their beds and exact paused care clocks. In models 1.2 and 1.3, a care stage already complete at the exact start of a disruption or supply depletion can finish and release its bed; new care remains blocked until recovery. Overlapping interruptions form a union. Previously saved dated pauses remain active and appear in a collapsed list below the arrivals chart, where individual pauses can be removed. Changing the daily chance requires a new independent scenario or observed reset rather than retroactively changing inherited simulated history. Unchanged chance settings remain compatible with inherited model 1.3 history. Date and refill scheduling use the hospital timezone.

Supplies use generic planning units, not separate item inventories. Starting stock is a percentage of maximum capacity. Modeled patients consume supplies only while receiving active care, at the configured units per patient-day. Regular refills add the configured percentage of capacity, capped at maximum capacity. Empty stock pauses care until stock becomes available. Results retain exact depletion/refill history, daily mean/minimum/end percentages and consumption/refill quantities. Weekly and monthly reductions sum quantities, retain the minimum and final stock, and weight average stock by actual elapsed time.

The four outcome cards show recorded average waiting per arriving patient, the most constrained department, average supply stock and a success score. All newly executed runs record waiting and success measures, even when optional operations settings are disabled; this passive accounting changes no existing trajectories or random draws. Untracked supply stock remains unavailable. Average waiting includes observed emergency and admission queues, including unfinished waiting up to the horizon, and excludes initial census. Supply percentages are time weighted. The success score is an access target: the percentage of eligible measured arrivals whose first treatment starts within 24 hours. Untreated patients with less than 24 hours of follow-up are excluded. No arrivals or eligible observations produce unavailable values. This score does not measure clinical outcomes.

Analysis → Summary places Decision summary above the charts. It uses the greatest recorded constraint burden and its most affected unit, patient queue and admission-wait measures, an exactly matched comparison when present, and a state-appropriate action. Technical definitions and uncertainty remain in “Why this conclusion?”. Unknown data never becomes a reported zero. Existing results without the new measures show unavailable values. A stale draft, incomplete comparison, unmatched revision/horizon/input set, failed result or missing paired evidence cannot support a new recommendation. A zero baseline does not produce an undefined percentage reduction. A ranking based on marginal medians is distinguished from median improvement in matched repetitions.

Compact constraint ledgers are integrated during every new simulation, even when a full trace is not retained. Ensemble summaries include proven zero observations only when evidence for every completed repetition is available. Any missing or malformed evidence leaves the ensemble unavailable. Legacy results fall back to an explicitly labeled retained example run. Constraint categories, candidate units and named resources can overlap and are never added as independent causes.

“View bottleneck” uses the recorded source repetition and timestamp. If its trace is not retained, the app requests and polls the existing reconstruction workflow before selecting it; switching context cancels the pending navigation. “Review response” opens the matching comparison with the recorded plan selected, reusing the existing save-plan flow. Analytics adds Medical supplies and a narrow ribbon above each plot: amber segments identify recorded resource constraints and red segments identify actual treatment interruptions. Adjacent recorded periods are joined; gaps remain visible. Hovering retains the exact supporting period and reasons. Ensemble marks indicate an observation in one or more simulations, not an interruption in every run. Period indicators are not presented as exact onset times or proof that arrivals alone caused a shortage.

Existing scenario records, saved results, workspace defaults and service volumes must remain unchanged during deployment. Acceptance evidence is recorded in `evidence/decision-operations/`; local execution uses an isolated database and artifact directory.

Operations models 1.0–1.2 remain available to reproduce existing frozen jobs, traces, exports and inherited simulated histories. Model 1.0 applied the severity duration across emergency and inpatient stages, including emergency-only visits, which could block assessment places for days. Model 1.1 corrected that severity interpretation; model 1.2 also corrects zero-remaining care at shortage boundaries. New independent runs use model 1.3, which adds daily disruption chance without altering absent/zero-chance trajectories. Old severity-enabled 1.0 conclusions remain explicitly labeled as an earlier model and require a new run before a response recommendation; saved results are never silently rewritten. Comparisons must also match the operations model version.

The simulation-logic audit covers exact resource-response cases, independently reconstructed patient/event metrics, API save/run/comparison lineage, and desktop Configuration → Summary → Analytics → Replay → Comparison flows. It includes7/14/30-day horizons, daylight-saving boundaries, zero resources, supply exhaustion/refill, overlapping disruptions, edited daily arrivals, unavailable metrics and historical model reproduction. Evidence is recorded in `evidence/simulation-logic-audit/`. These finite checks validate modeled planning rules; they do not calibrate the synthetic parameters against hospital observations.
