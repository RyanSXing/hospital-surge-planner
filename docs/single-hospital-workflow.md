# Single-hospital planning workflow

The workspace now focuses on one hospital. **Configuration** shows the starting census and the simulation setup. **Analysis** contains Summary, Replay, Analytics and Comparison. Analytics has Outcomes and Patients & units. The separate Resources workspace and alternate-site/transfer planning controls have been removed from the current workflow.

1. Open **demo** on Configuration. Choose a duration, adjust emergency arrivals, and choose whether the increase is steady, builds up, or rises and falls. Drag a blue point up or down to change that day’s arrivals, or use the initial arrival presets. The chart updates immediately and compares planned arrivals with usual demand. Daily edits become a custom curve and support one-step undo. It is a demand preview; computed occupancy and queues appear after a simulation.
2. In **Beds & staffing**, choose a hospital unit and set total beds, the staffed-bed limit, registered nurses, doctors/providers and support staff per shift. Editing the staffed-bed limit adjusts the nurse count; the readback shows the capacity actually supported by staff and equipment. These are scenario assumptions; the source baseline remains unchanged. Open **More parameters** only when needed for exact timing, existing care-mix settings, staff absences, readiness, equipment, run counts and validation. These changes are saved in canonical scenario revisions. Empty assumption tracks and raw JSON editing are no longer part of setup.
3. Select **Run simulation**, then review Summary. Its four outcome cards match Analytics: new arrivals, completed discharges, placement waiting and ward utilization. The full-width forecast timeline lets you choose a day or exact time. A single Week/Month interval appears as an observed point rather than an empty trend. **What limited patient flow** names recorded bed, staffing and equipment constraints, their first occurrence and waiting exposure for the selected run. Causes may overlap.
4. Open Replay to follow patients through their recorded care stages. Playback buffers upcoming states and transition events, including short visits between snapshots. Discharged patients move to the discharge bay before their markers leave. Pause and seek reconstruct the exact displayed time. Patient and event details are optional.
5. In **Analytics → Outcomes**, click a chart bookmark to switch among arrivals, occupancy, waiting, staffing/equipment deficits and daily capacity. One full-size chart preserves the selected time and offers exact chart data.
6. Use Comparison to evaluate reserve staffing, opening hospital beds, or opening and staffing beds. New space is inside Ward C, and opening space does not manufacture staff or equipment. Save a selected response as a new scenario when useful.

Staff, equipment and beds can be inspected through focused search dialogs or unit details. Recording a change still creates a new forecast branch and preserves completed inputs. Data & setup and exports remain secondary tools. Legacy Resources links resolve to Patients & units; existing records and model artifacts remain readable.

The new immutable `hospital_baseline` fixture contains one hospital, five units, 84 starting patients, 100 installed ward beds (80 supported by staff), 12 ICU beds, and 30 emergency places (24 supported by staff). Ward C has 12 additional planned bed spaces. Reserve staff and equipment retain real allocation limits. The starter **demo** uses seven days, ten runs, and normal arrivals.

The geographic backdrop is centered at Vanderbilt University Hospital. Patient, staffing, demand and internal unit data are generated planning inputs, not Vanderbilt operational records. Provenance is available in model details and source metadata rather than persistent page badges.

The earlier requested workspace reset archived prior scenarios recoverably. This Configuration/Analysis update preserves existing scenarios, completed runs and workspace defaults. Verification targets the desktop interface; mobile support is outside the current scope.
